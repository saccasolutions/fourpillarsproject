# Transition Plan: From Current System to Four Pillars
### Year-by-Year Implementation Roadmap
**Author:** Rob DeMaria | **Version:** 1.0 | **Date:** April 18, 2026

---

## Guiding Principle

No one loses income during the transition. Current benefit recipients keep their benefits until the new system provides more. Changes are phased, data-driven, and reversible at each stage. The system proves itself before scaling up.

---

## Pre-Launch: Legislative & Infrastructure (18-24 months)

**Legislative action required:**
- Omnibus UBI Act establishing the Four Pillars framework, floor structure, and tax changes
- VAT implementation legislation (exempt categories, collection mechanisms, state coordination)
- Automation Displacement Tax Act (defining displacement, measurement standards, reporting requirements)
- Corporate tax rate restoration to 28%
- Employer FICA cap removal and rate adjustment to 9%
- Investment income tax at 25% (separated from eliminated income tax)
- 401(k) mandate legislation
- Healthcare gap coverage establishment

**Infrastructure buildout:**
- VAT collection system deployment (point-of-sale integration, business registration)
- UBI payment system (direct deposit infrastructure for 258M adults, identity verification)
- Automation tax reporting framework (IRS guidance on labor displacement measurement)
- 401(k) custodian partnerships (Vanguard, Fidelity, Schwab — bulk account creation)
- Child seed account program design
- Pillar 2-4 measurement systems (data integration with existing platforms)

## Year 1: Launch

**What happens:**
- Federal income tax eliminated. First paycheck of the year arrives with zero federal withholding.
- Employee FICA eliminated. Additional ~7.65% in every paycheck.
- 20% VAT goes live. Businesses collect at point of sale. Basics exempt.
- UBI floor payments begin: $25,000/year, phased (full below $30K earnings, phases out by $80K).
- Employer FICA adjusted to 9%, wage cap removed.
- Corporate tax rate moves to 28%.
- Investment income tax (25%) goes live.
- Financial transaction tax (0.25%) goes live.
- All other new taxes take effect.
- Employer insurance stays. Government healthcare for gaps begins enrolling.

**What doesn't happen yet:**
- Social Security benefits continue for ALL current recipients — no changes.
- SS/FICA employee contributions were funding SS — now redirected to 401(k). Current SS recipients funded from general UBI revenue (floor payments to retirees serve this function).
- Automation tax begins at reported displacement levels (~10%).
- Pillar 2-4 payments begin at conservative levels while measurement systems calibrate.

**Budget impact (Year 1):**
- Outflows: ~$9.82T
- Revenue: ~$7.69T
- Gap: ~$2.13T (vs. $1.30T current deficit)

**What people experience:**
- Bigger paychecks (no income tax, no FICA)
- Floor payments arriving monthly via direct deposit
- Higher prices on non-essential goods (20% VAT)
- Net effect: every income level better off (see persona analysis)

## Year 2: Calibration

**Adjustments based on Year 1 data:**
- VAT collection efficiency measured. Exempt categories refined if needed.
- Automation tax compliance assessed. Reporting standards adjusted.
- Floor phase-out mechanics verified — are there perverse incentives? Edge cases?
- Pillar 2-4 measurement systems refined using real participation data.
- Inflation monitoring — is the 3-5% short-term bump materializing? Standard monetary policy response if so.

**New this year:**
- Mandatory 401(k) enrollment begins for all workers under 30 (new entrants to workforce).
- Child seed accounts launched for all newborns.
- Automation tax rate adjustments based on actual displacement data.

**Budget impact (Year 2):**
- Automation tax growing as reporting matures: ~$0.87T
- GDP growth from Year 1 stimulus beginning to show in VAT revenue
- Gap narrowing: ~$1.79T

---

## Year 3: Social Security Transition Begins

**SS transition — Phase 1:**
- Workers aged 18-35: fully transition to mandatory 401(k). No further SS credits accumulated.
- Workers aged 36-50: hybrid. 50% of employer FICA funds UBI system, 50% funds a transitional personal account. SS benefits earned to date are preserved.
- Workers aged 51+: no change. Continue earning SS credits. Benefits fully honored.
- Current SS recipients: benefits continue at current levels, funded from general revenue (their UBI floor replaces the equivalent amount).

**New this year:**
- Employer insurance backstop penalty enforced. Companies without qualifying coverage now paying 13-14% FICA.
- Pillar 2-4 payment scales published — transparent formulas for creative, civic, and family contributions.
- Corporate minimum tax enforcement begins.

**Budget impact (Year 3):**
- Revenue: ~$8.38T (automation tax at ~$1.05T, VAT growing with GDP)
- Gap: ~$1.44T (approaching current deficit level)

---

## Year 4: Maturation

**System stabilization:**
- Inflation from Year 1 launch has normalized (per historical pattern of redistribution-based transfers).
- Labor market adjusted to new incentives. Employment data shows impact of floor on workforce participation.
- Automation tax revenue accelerating as AI deployment expands across industries.
- First child seed accounts showing compound growth (small but symbolic).

**Data-driven decisions:**
- Is the floor phase-out rate (50¢/$1) optimal? Adjust if data shows work disincentives at any threshold.
- Is the automation tax rate (50%) the right balance between revenue and innovation incentive? Adjust if needed.
- Pillar 2-4 participation rates — are the right behaviors being incentivized?

**Budget impact (Year 4):**
- Revenue: ~$8.75T
- Gap: ~$1.07T — now BELOW the current federal deficit

---

## Year 5: Floor Scaling — Phase 1

**Floor increases from $25,000 to $28,000.** This is the first scaling event, triggered by:
- Revenue trajectory showing path to balance
- Automation tax exceeding $1.5T
- GDP growth confirming consumer spending boost
- Inflation within acceptable range

**SS transition — Phase 2:**
- Workers aged 36-50 from Year 3 transition: now 39-53. Shift to 75% personal account / 25% SS transition.
- Workers who were 51+ in Year 3: now 54+. Still earning SS credits if under retirement age. Those at retirement age receiving full SS or UBI floor (whichever higher).

**Budget impact (Year 5):**
- Outflows increase: ~$10.38T (higher floor)
- Revenue: ~$9.53T (automation at ~$1.63T)
- Gap: ~$0.85T

---

## Year 6-7: Approaching Balance

**Year 6:**
- Revenue: ~$9.94T
- Gap: ~$0.44T — well below current deficit
- Automation tax at ~$1.85T and growing

**Year 7:**
- Revenue: ~$10.38T
- **Gap: ~$0.00T — BUDGET BALANCE**
- First year the system potentially runs at equilibrium
- Automation tax at ~$2.08T

**Milestones:**
- Full SS transition for workers under 40 (now 40-43 from Year 3 cohort).
- Pillar 2-4 payment scales mature. Real data replaces estimated payment levels.
- First cohort of child seed accounts reaching meaningful balances ($3,500-$12,000 at age 7).

## Year 8: Floor Scaling — Phase 2

**Floor increases from $28,000 to $30,000.** Original target floor achieved, triggered by:
- System at or near budget balance for 1+ years
- Automation tax exceeding $2T
- GDP growth compound effect fully realized

**Budget impact (Year 8):**
- Outflows: ~$10.75T
- Revenue: ~$10.83T
- **Surplus: ~$0.08T — first surplus**

## Year 9-10: Surplus Era

**Year 9:**
- Revenue: ~$11.30T
- Surplus: ~$0.55T
- National debt reduction begins

**Year 10:**
- Revenue: ~$11.78T
- Surplus: ~$1.03T
- Policy choices: increase floor beyond $30K? Accelerate debt paydown? Sovereign wealth fund?

**SS transition complete (for new entrants):**
- Everyone who entered the workforce after Year 1 has a personal 401(k) only.
- Workers 54+ at launch (now 64+) transitioning to retirement. Receiving whichever is higher: earned SS benefit or UBI floor.
- Within 20-25 years of launch, Social Security fully sunsets as the last pre-transition retirees pass through the system.

## Transition Protections

### For Current Retirees
- SS benefits continue at current levels, adjusted for COLAs as they are today.
- UBI floor provides additional income on top of SS (or replaces SS if floor exceeds benefit).
- Healthcare coverage continues through government gap plan (replacing Medicare).
- No retiree experiences an income decrease at any point in the transition.

### For Mid-Career Workers (35-55 at launch)
- All SS benefits earned to date are preserved.
- Gradual transition from SS contributions to personal 401(k).
- At retirement, receive: preserved SS benefit + 401(k) balance + UBI floor + pillar credits.
- This cohort has the BEST of both worlds during transition — some SS + growing 401(k).

### For Young Workers (18-35 at launch)
- Immediate full transition to 401(k).
- 38+ years of compound growth ahead.
- Child seed accounts for their children from birth.
- This cohort retires with the strongest financial position in American history.

### For Companies
- Year 1 changes are straightforward: adjust FICA rate, no cap, begin VAT collection.
- Automation tax reporting requirements phased — Year 1 is reporting only (no tax); Year 2 begins at 25%; Year 3+ at full 50%. This gives companies time to build compliance systems.
- Health insurance requirements unchanged in Year 1. Penalty backstop phased in by Year 3.

---

## Rollback Provisions

If at any point during Phase 1-3 the system produces unacceptable outcomes (sustained inflation above 6%, GDP contraction, employment collapse), specific components can be adjusted without unwinding the entire framework:
- Floor amount can be reduced
- VAT rate can be adjusted up or down
- Automation tax rate can be modified
- Phase-out thresholds can be changed
- Individual components are independent — adjusting one doesn't break the others

The framework is designed as a set of modular, adjustable levers — not an all-or-nothing system.
