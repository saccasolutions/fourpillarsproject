# Persona Stress Tests: Four Pillars UBI Framework (v3)
### Systematic Evaluation Against 17 Real-World Personas
**Author:** Analysis prepared for Rob DeMaria | **Date:** April 18, 2026
**Framework Version:** 3.0 — Phased floor, employer insurance, complete federal balance sheet

---

## Methodology

Each persona is evaluated against the four pillars (Economic, Creative/Cultural, Civic/Community, Family/Generational) plus the phased floor. Framework v3 key parameters:

- **Floor:** Up to $25,000/year. Full below $30K earnings, phases at 50¢ per $1 above $30K, gone at $80K. Scaling to $30K as revenue permits.
- **Pillar 1:** GDP-linked market compensation — **paid by employers, not the government**
- **No income tax / no employee FICA** — gross = take-home (minus VAT on spending)
- **20% VAT** on non-exempt spending (groceries, rent, healthcare, education exempt)
- **Employer health insurance** continues; government covers gaps
- **Eligibility:** US citizens only
- **Dual-pillar attribution:** allowed
- **Automation:** ghost salaries fund the system via 50% automation tax
- **Retirement:** mandatory 10% 401(k) + child seed accounts replace Social Security
- **Incarceration:** UBI funds costs + trust fund + restitution/child support

**VAT estimates** assume spending rates of 85-90% for low income, 70-80% for middle, 60% for high income, with 30% of spending exempt.

## Persona 1: Retired Teacher (Margaret, 67)

**Situation:** Retired after 38 years teaching 3rd grade. Suburban Ohio. Tutors neighborhood kids 2-3 afternoons/week. Active in church. Two grown children, both college-educated professionals. Husband deceased.

**Pillar Mapping:**
- **Economic:** $0 (retired)
- **Creative:** ~$500 (minimal)
- **Civic:** ~$5,000 (tutoring, church, community — 8-10 hrs/week)
- **Family:** ~$10,000 (raised two successful adults — outcome-based credit)

**Framework Income:**
| Component | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| Floor (earns $0 → full) | $25,000 |
| Pillar 2 (Creative) | $500 |
| Pillar 3 (Civic) | $5,000 |
| Pillar 4 (Family) | $10,000 |
| **UBI Gross** | **$40,500** |
| Estimated VAT | -$3,570 |
| **UBI Net** | **$36,930** |
| 401(k) withdrawal (38 yrs @ 7%, 10% contribution on $65K) | ~$46,400 |
| **Grand Total** | **~$83,330 (no income tax)** |

**vs. Current System:** SS benefit (~$28-32K) + pension (~$15-25K) = ~$43-57K gross, ~$38-48K after tax.

**Assessment:** Margaret is significantly better off. Her 401(k) built from mandatory 10% contributions over 38 years provides $46K/year — exceeding SS alone. Combined with the full floor and pillar credits, she's at ~$83K vs ~$43K. She owns her retirement savings. The floor at $25K (vs $30K in v2) is offset by realistic 401(k) projections and zero income tax.

## Key Findings: v3.0 vs. v2.0

**What changed from v2 to v3:**

1. **Floor is phased, not universal.** Instead of a flat $30K for everyone, the floor is $25K maximum and phases out above $30K earnings. This reduces system cost from $7.74T to $4.62T while still reaching 90% of adults. High earners (NBA Star, CEO, AI Engineer, Senior Manager) receive $0 floor — but gain substantially from no income tax.

2. **Healthcare stays with employers.** Instead of universal single-payer ($3.1T), employer insurance continues and the government covers gaps ($1.55T). No disruption for the ~160M Americans already covered through work.

3. **Companies pay more.** Corporate tax at 28%, automation tax at 50%, employer FICA at 9% with no wage cap. Company share of federal revenue goes from 16% to 37%.

4. **The math is honest.** v2 showed a $2.54T gap without accounting for remaining federal operations. v3 includes the complete federal balance sheet — all outflows, all revenue — showing a $2.13T gap that closes through automation tax growth over ~7-10 years.

5. **Every persona still comes out ahead** (or equivalent) when all income sources are considered. The framework delivers material improvement for every citizen while running a gap smaller than the current deficit's trajectory.
