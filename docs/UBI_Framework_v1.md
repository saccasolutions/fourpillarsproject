# The Four Pillars UBI Framework
### A Contribution-Based Universal Basic Income for a Post-Scarcity World
**Author:** Rob DeMaria | **Version:** 3.0 | **Date:** April 18, 2026

---

## The Core Idea

Standard UBI treats everyone the same — a reclusive billionaire and a devoted community nurse get the same check. This framework replaces that with a system that rewards meaningful participation in human life.

Every adult US citizen receives a guaranteed floor. On top of that, four independent pillars of human contribution determine how much additional prosperity you earn. There is no ceiling. The system doesn't punish success — it redefines what counts as contribution.

This isn't a safety net. It's a fundamental economic redesign for a world where AI makes human labor optional but human contribution remains central to a good life.

---

## Eligibility

**US citizens only.** The Four Pillars framework applies exclusively to adult citizens of the United States (18+). This is a bright line — no ambiguity, no tiers, no exceptions. Immigration policy is a separate question; this framework defines how American citizens share in American prosperity.

Children do not receive the floor directly. They are covered within their family unit's economic calculation through the Family pillar, and through child seed investment accounts (see Retirement & Investment section below).

---

## The Universal Floor

**Up to $25,000/year for every adult US citizen (18+), scaling to $30,000 as revenue permits.**

The floor phases with income to focus resources where they matter most while keeping the system universal — 90% of adult citizens receive some floor payment. The phase-out is gradual enough that earning more always leaves you better off.

### Phase-Out Structure

- **Earning $0 – $30,000:** Full $25,000 floor. No reduction.
- **Earning $30,001 – $79,999:** Floor reduces by 50 cents for every dollar earned above $30,000. A person earning $50,000 receives a $15,000 floor payment. A person earning $70,000 receives $5,000.
- **Earning $80,000+:** Floor fully phased out. These individuals gain substantially from the elimination of income tax and employee payroll taxes.

### Why $30K Before Phase-Out?

The $30,000 threshold ensures that every worker earning at or below the median individual income receives the full floor. Part-time workers, caregivers, artists, and entry-level employees are fully supported. The phase-out only begins for people earning enough to absorb the reduction comfortably.

### Why This Is Still "Universal"

Everyone is eligible. The phase-out is mechanical and based on earnings, not means-tested. Nobody fills out an application. Nobody proves they're poor. The floor shows up automatically — more for people who need it, less for people who don't, nothing for people who are already well-compensated. 90% of adult citizens receive a payment.

### Floor Scaling

The income floor does not replace disability benefits (SSDI), Medicaid, or specialized care programs — those continue unchanged. The floor replaces only general income support programs (SNAP, TANF, SSI) that provide less than $25,000 annually; all recipients are made whole or better off.

The floor launches at $25,000 and scales toward $30,000 as the automation tax base grows. Every $1,000 increase in the floor costs approximately $230 billion. The 10-year growth trajectory projects the system reaching surplus by Year 10 (56% probability per extended Monte Carlo simulation; 80% by Year 20), at which point the floor can scale to $28,000–$30,000. The system tells you when it can afford more — no guesswork required.

The floor is also the system's built-in fraud prevention: when people aren't desperate, they don't need to game the system. Trust starts here.

## The Four Pillars

Each pillar is independent. Each carries equal weight. Contributions across any combination of pillars can take a person from the floor to genuine affluence. **There is no ceiling.**

**Dual-pillar attribution is allowed and expected.** If someone genuinely contributes across multiple pillars — an athlete's performance is both economic activity and cultural contribution, a teacher's work is both economic and civic — they are credited in every applicable pillar. That's the point of having four independent dimensions. Human contribution is multidimensional; the framework reflects that.

### Pillar 1: Economic Contribution

Your market compensation for economic activity.

Pillar 1 is what the market already does — employers paying people based on their economic impact. The framework simply names it and ties it to GDP growth. **Employers pay Pillar 1, not the government.** It is not a federal expenditure.

The formula:

> **Pillar 1 Payment = Market Role Compensation × GDP Growth Factor**

Where:
- **Market Role Compensation** = what the market currently pays for your role. For employees: salary/wages (W-2). For self-employed: business income (1099/Schedule C). For gig workers: platform earnings. For athletes/performers: contract + performance-based pay.
- **GDP Growth Factor** = Current GDP / Base Year GDP. Launches at 1.0. If GDP grows 3%, everyone's Pillar 1 grows 3%. If GDP contracts, Pillar 1 shrinks (but the floor holds).

**Key properties:**
- Transparent: your compensation is derived from a formula, not backroom negotiation
- GDP-linked: as national prosperity grows, everyone's income grows proportionally
- Performance-driven: an NBA player filling arenas earns more than one on an empty bench. A CEO growing the company earns more than one shrinking it. The market already prices this; the formula just makes the GDP link explicit.
- Uses existing infrastructure: tax records, employer data, business filings
- No ceiling: a founder whose company represents a significant GDP share earns proportionally
- **Not a government cost:** Pillar 1 flows through the private economy. The GDP Growth Factor is a tracking mechanism and aspirational target, not a government check.

**What about automation?** When robots replace workers, the displaced workers' "ghost salaries" do NOT get absorbed by the remaining employees. Those labor cost savings flow to the automation tax (see Funding section), which funds the UBI floor for displaced workers. The remaining workers earn Pillar 1 based on their own role's market compensation — not inflated by the robots beside them. The many humans who designed, built, and maintain the automation are credited through their own companies' Pillar 1 contributions.

**Wage suppression protection:** Companies might try to lower salaries knowing workers have a floor payment. The primary defense is labor market competition — companies still compete for talent. The floor actually strengthens workers' bargaining position: they can walk away from bad offers. The GDP tracking framework provides data to identify systematic wage suppression by sector, enabling targeted policy responses if needed. Minimum wage laws remain as a backstop and can be indexed to inflation.

### Pillar 2: Creative & Cultural Contribution

Arts, music, innovation, storytelling, invention, spiritual leadership — the things that make life meaningful rather than just functional.

This pillar is market-informed but not market-limited. A musician who plays live shows twice a week gets a baseline credit for keeping human culture alive. But if that musician builds an audience, streams millions, becomes the next Michael Jackson — their Creative contribution scales just like Economic contribution. The system doesn't cap creative success.

**How it's measured (existing data first, attestation for the gaps):**
- Streaming numbers, performance calendars, exhibition records
- Patent filings, published works, public performances
- Sales and audience metrics for content creators
- Community attestation only for contributions without existing paper trails

### Pillar 3: Civic & Community Contribution

Volunteering, organizing, mentoring, local leadership, public service, neighborhood involvement — the social glue that holds communities together.

Someone who volunteers every day helping the homeless, feeding the sick, working in shelters — that's just as valuable as raising a child. This pillar recognizes that communities don't function without people who show up for each other.

**How it's measured (existing data first, attestation for the gaps):**
- Volunteer hour logs through organizations (food banks, shelters, nonprofits already track this)
- Coaching records (leagues and schools document this)
- Participation in local government, civic organizations, boards
- Mentorship programs, community leadership roles
- Community attestation for informal contributions without paper trails

### Pillar 4: Family & Generational Contribution

Raising children, caregiving, building family stability, continuity of culture across generations.

This is distinct from Economic contribution — it's not just unpaid labor that feeds the economy. It's its own category of human contribution. Raising humans, building resilience, creating stability. Family contribution stands on its own merit, separate from whether it shows up in GDP.

**Critical design choice: quality over quantity.** Five neglected children do not equal more credit than one child who is homeschooled, gets straight A's, does dance and gymnastics, and is raised well. The system measures parenting outcomes and effort, not headcount.

**How it's measured (existing data first, attestation for the gaps):**
- Children's school grades and performance (already in school systems)
- Health outcomes and doctor visit records
- Dependents on tax returns, guardianship records
- Caregiving hours for elderly or disabled family members (medical records)
- Community attestation only for aspects without existing documentation

## Measurement Philosophy

The four pillars use **existing data infrastructure wherever possible** and reserve community attestation for the gaps.

**Economic:** Hard data. Tax records, employer data, GDP attribution. All infrastructure already exists.

**Creative:** Primarily market data — streaming platforms, venue records, patent offices, publishing records, sales metrics. Community attestation only for creative work that doesn't generate market data (community muralist, local theater, etc.).

**Civic:** Organization records — volunteer hours logged by nonprofits, coaching records from leagues, board service documentation. Most civic contribution is already documented by the organizations where it happens. Community attestation fills gaps for informal contributions.

**Family:** School records, health records, tax filings. A child's grades are a direct measure of parenting investment. Coaching shows up in league records. Community attestation is reserved for things without paper trails — like being the neighbor who watches kids after school.

**Key insight:** The system doesn't need to measure everything perfectly. The floor means nobody is desperate enough to fabricate civic hours or fake parenting outcomes. The measurement system needs to be "good enough" — not surveillance-grade.

## Incarceration Policy

Incarcerated people are still citizens and still human. The floor still applies to them. However, during incarceration, the floor is allocated as follows:

1. **System costs:** A portion covers the cost of incarceration (housing, food, medical) — costs the system bears anyway.
2. **Obligations:** Any outstanding child support or victim restitution is paid from the UBI before other allocations.
3. **Trust fund:** The remainder accumulates in a supervised trust account, building a real landing pad for release.

This is not punishment. It's practical management of someone's UBI during a period when they can't freely participate in society. When Derek serves 7 years, he comes out with a meaningful savings cushion instead of $50 and a bus ticket. That's better for reducing recidivism than anything the current system offers.

Post-release, full floor and pillar eligibility resumes immediately.

## Healthcare

### Structure: Employer Insurance + Government Safety Net

The framework does not nationalize healthcare. Employer-provided health insurance remains the primary coverage mechanism for working Americans and their families. Companies continue to provide insurance as they do today.

The government fills the gaps — covering populations that don't have access to employer plans:
- **Retirees** (replacing Medicare)
- **Low-income individuals** (replacing Medicaid)
- **Self-employed and gig workers** without employer coverage
- **Unemployed** citizens between jobs
- **Anyone** whose employer doesn't offer qualifying insurance

**Estimated government healthcare cost: ~$1.55 trillion** — covering approximately 100 million people at ~$15,500 per person. This replaces $1.8T in current Medicare, Medicaid, CHIP, and other health/welfare program spending.

### Employer Insurance Backstop

To prevent companies from dropping insurance to push workers onto the government plan, an employer mandate with real teeth is built into the payroll tax structure:

- **Companies that provide qualifying insurance:** Standard employer FICA rate of 9%
- **Companies that don't provide insurance:** FICA rate jumps to 13-14%, with the premium funding those workers' government coverage

This makes it cheaper for companies to provide insurance than to pay the penalty. Either way, the money shows up. Companies that game the system subsidize the coverage they're trying to avoid.

## Retirement & Investment Model

**Social Security is replaced** by personal investment accounts, not by government promises.

### Mandatory 401(k)-Style Accounts

Workers contribute a mandatory percentage of their earnings to personal investment accounts. With no income tax and no employee FICA, take-home pay is substantially higher — workers can afford the contribution and still keep more than they do today.

- **Contribution:** Mandatory 10% of earnings into personal 401(k)
- **Investment:** Standard market index funds, age-appropriate allocation
- **Ownership:** This is YOUR money. Not a government promise — actual assets you own.
- **Employer match:** Encouraged but not mandated by the framework

**Example — Teacher earning $65,000:**
- Annual 401(k) contribution: $6,500 (10% mandatory)
- After 38 years at 7% average return: **$1.16 million**
- 4% safe withdrawal rate: **$46,400/year** in retirement income
- Plus UBI floor (full $25K at retirement if not working): **$25,000**
- Total retirement income: **$71,400/year**
- Compare to current SS benefit for teachers: **$28,000–$32,000/year**

The framework dramatically outperforms Social Security for retirement, and the money belongs to the individual.

### Child Seed Investment Accounts

Every child born to US citizen parents receives a seed investment account at birth. This leverages the most powerful force in finance: compound interest starting from day zero.

**Projections (8% average market return):**

| Seed Amount | Annual Add | Age 18 | Age 25 | Age 65 |
|-------------|-----------|--------|--------|--------|
| $1,000 | $500/yr | $22,700 | $43,400 | $1,072,000 |
| $5,000 | $1,000/yr | $57,400 | $107,300 | $2,591,000 |

A $5,000 seed with $1,000/year builds to $57,000 by college age and over $2.5 million by retirement — without the individual contributing a dime from their own earnings.

Combined with mandatory 401(k) contributions from their first job, retirement becomes individually owned and compounding-powered. No government promise needed.

## Tax Structure

The framework fundamentally simplifies the tax system for individuals: **eliminate taxes on earning, implement taxes on consuming.** Companies contribute through payroll taxes, automation taxes, and corporate income taxes.

### Taxes Eliminated (for individuals)
- **Federal income tax** — people keep 100% of what they earn
- **Employee FICA/SS tax** (6.2%) — eliminated entirely
- **Employee Medicare tax** (1.45%) — eliminated entirely
- **Complex deductions, credits, and annual filing** — eliminated for most people

Total eliminated: **$3.45 trillion** in individual tax burden removed.

### Taxes Kept (modified)
- **Employer FICA** — rate adjusted to 9%, wage cap removed. Employers pay on full payroll. Revenue: ~$0.99T
- **Corporate income tax** — rate restored to 28% (pre-2017 level). Revenue: ~$0.84T
- **Excise taxes and tariffs** — kept as-is. Revenue: ~$0.30T

### New Taxes Implemented
- **Consumption/VAT tax (20%)** — paid at point of sale, not on income. Basic necessities (groceries, rent, healthcare, education, childcare) are exempt. The floor acts as a built-in "prebate" making the VAT progressive. Revenue: ~$3.76T (including circular flow recapture)
- **Automation displacement tax (50%)** — when companies save on labor costs by replacing workers with AI/automation, half of those net savings is taxed. This is the funding centerpiece: the very force displacing workers funds their prosperity. Still profitable for companies — save $1M in labor, keep $500K. Revenue: ~$0.71T at launch, growing to $2-3T over a decade
- **Investment income tax (25%)** — on capital gains, dividends, and interest income. This is NOT a tax on wages — workers keep 100% of their paycheck. This taxes passive investment returns at roughly the same rate investors pay today (current top rate is 23.8%). Without this, eliminating income tax would accidentally make capital gains tax-free — a massive gift to the wealthy. Revenue: ~$0.41T
- **Financial transaction tax (0.25%)** — on stock, bond, and derivatives trades. Exists in 40+ countries. Revenue: ~$0.20T
- **Wealth tax (2% on net worth above $1 billion)** — modest, not punitive. A billionaire with $5B pays 2% on $4B = $80M/year. They still have $4.92B. No ceiling violated. Not aimed at millionaires. Revenue: ~$0.10T
- **Corporate minimum tax (15%)** — no company earning over $1B in profits pays less than 15% effective rate, regardless of deductions. Revenue: ~$0.13T
- **Digital advertising/data monetization tax (5-10%)** — companies profiting from user data contribute. Revenue: ~$0.06T
- **Stock buyback tax (4%)** — companies spent ~$1T on buybacks last year. Money going to shareholders instead of workers should contribute. Revenue: ~$0.04T
- **Estate tax (reformed)** — exemption lowered to $5M, trust loopholes closed. Revenue: ~$0.08T
- **Employer insurance penalty** — companies without qualifying health insurance pay a FICA surcharge. Revenue: ~$0.08T

### Funding Math

**Annual outflows:**
- UBI Floor (phased, $25K max): $4.62T
- Pillar 2-4 payments: $0.80T
- Government healthcare (gaps): $1.55T
- Administration: $0.05T
- Federal operations (defense, debt service, discretionary): $2.80T
- **Total: ~$9.82T**

Note: Pillar 1 is NOT a system cost — it's market compensation paid by employers.

**Annual revenue:**
- Kept taxes (employer FICA, corporate, excise): $2.13T
- VAT at 20% (with circular flow): $3.76T
- New sources (automation, investment, FTT, etc.): $1.80T
- **Total: ~$7.69T**

**Gap: ~$2.13T** — compared to the current federal deficit of $1.30T.

### Who Pays What

- **Companies pay 37%** of total revenue ($2.84T) — up from 16% today. This includes employer FICA, corporate tax, automation tax, corporate minimum tax, digital tax, buyback tax, and payroll penalties.
- **Individuals pay 57%** of total revenue ($4.35T) — primarily through the 20% VAT on spending plus investment income tax, wealth tax, and estate tax on the wealthy.
- **Mixed sources: 6%** ($0.50T)

The company share more than doubles from 16% to 37% while every individual income level comes out ahead.

### Why the Math Gets Better Every Year

**The self-reinforcing loop:** More automation → more automation tax revenue → higher UBI → more consumer spending → higher GDP → larger VAT base → more funding. The automation tax alone is projected to grow from $0.71T at launch to $2-3T within a decade as AI deployment accelerates.

**10-year trajectory:** The system reaches projected budget balance by Year 9-10 at 3% GDP growth and 2% annual increase in automation displacement. At that point, revenue exceeds spending, enabling either floor increases (toward $30K), national debt reduction, or both.

## Design Principles

These are non-negotiable constraints on every aspect of the system.

**1. Prosperity, not survival.** The goal is not a safety net. People should be able to reach upper-middle-class or wealthy through pillar contributions.

**2. No punishment mechanics.** The system rewards contribution. It does not penalize behavior or lifestyle choices.

**3. No ceiling.** The system must still produce Elon Musks and Michael Jacksons. Massive contribution in any pillar earns proportionally massive income.

**4. Trust humans by default.** People game systems out of desperation. A solid floor means no one needs to lie just to survive.

**5. Quality over quantity.** Especially in the Family pillar. Outcomes and genuine effort matter more than raw numbers.

**6. US citizens only.** Clear, simple eligibility. No ambiguity.

**7. Own your retirement.** Personal investment accounts, not government promises. Compound interest from birth.

**8. Tax consumption, not income.** People keep what they earn. The system funds itself through spending, automation savings, corporate contribution, and economic growth.

**9. Companies pay their fair share.** Businesses benefit enormously from a stable, healthy, spending consumer base. Their revenue contribution should reflect that — 37% of federal revenue, up from 16%.

**10. The system improves itself.** Automation drives funding growth. GDP growth drives VAT growth. The framework is designed to become MORE sustainable over time, not less.

## What Was Considered and Rejected

**Universal (non-phased) floor.** A flat $25-30K to every adult regardless of income costs $6.45-7.74T. The phased approach costs $4.62T while still reaching 90% of adults, focusing resources where they matter most.

**Universal single-payer healthcare.** At $3.1T in federal cost, it was the most expensive healthcare option. Keeping employer insurance and covering gaps costs $1.55T — saving $1.55T while disrupting nothing for the ~160M Americans already covered through work.

**Carbon tax.** Rejected.

**Climate/Ecological as a pillar.** Rejected because it introduces punishment mechanics.

**Stewardship & Wisdom as a separate pillar.** Folded into Civic and Creative.

**Health & Wellbeing as a pillar.** Repositioned as a prerequisite, not a pillar. "Gating, not grading."

**Children receiving UBI directly.** Rejected — children are covered through the Family pillar and child seed investment accounts.

**Flat percentage multipliers for non-economic pillars.** Rejected in favor of impact-based scaling.

**Primary/secondary pillar attribution limits.** Rejected — if contribution spans multiple pillars, credit all of them. That's the point.

**Lifetime contribution credit for retirement.** Rejected in favor of personal investment accounts (mandatory 401k + child seed accounts). Individuals own their retirement savings rather than relying on a government credit system.

**Ultra-high wealth taxes on millionaires.** Rejected as punitive and inconsistent with "no ceiling." Modest 2% tax on billionaires only.

**Income tax (modified or replaced).** Eliminated entirely for individuals. Investment income tax (25%) retained to prevent untaxed capital gains — roughly matching current rates.

**Ghost salary attribution to remaining workers.** Rejected — when robots replace workers, the savings fund the system through automation tax, not inflated pay for the few remaining humans.

**Higher VAT rate.** Kept at 20% to protect consumers. Revenue gaps are closed through company-side taxes (automation, corporate, FICA adjustments) rather than higher consumer burden.

## Open Questions for Future Work

1. **Pillar 1 GDP Growth Factor enforcement.** The GDP Growth Factor tracks whether compensation keeps pace with national prosperity. What triggers a policy response if the ratio drifts? Advisory mechanism vs. regulatory mandate?

2. **VAT implementation details.** Exactly which categories are exempt? How does state sales tax interact? Implementation timeline for point-of-sale systems.

3. **Automation tax measurement.** How do you measure "labor cost savings from automation" precisely? What counts as automation vs. normal productivity improvement? Compliance infrastructure for reporting.

4. **Creative pillar scaling.** Where's the line between hobby and contribution? How do we avoid the government deciding what's "real art"?

5. **Family pillar outcome timing.** When do parenting outcomes "count"? Should a retiree whose adult children are thriving still receive Family credit?

6. **SS transition mechanics.** Current recipients stay on SS. Mid-career workers need a transition window. How long? What about the existing SS trust fund?

7. **Child seed account structure.** Government-managed fund or individual brokerage accounts? What investment options? Can parents contribute additional amounts?

8. **Pilot program design.** City or state selection, scope, which pillars to test first. Floor + Pillar 1 can launch independently.

9. **Floor scaling triggers.** What revenue milestones trigger floor increases from $25K toward $30K? Automatic mechanism vs. legislative action?
